# Investment Framework

Version: 1.0  
Date: 2026-06-04

## Fundamental Belief

Trading is cognition monetization.

Whether intraday, swing, or long-term, the essence is using cognitive difference to obtain return.

## Portfolio Role Framework

Every position must have a role before action.

Default roles:

- long-term core holding
- swing holding
- intraday holding
- hand-feel trade
- leveraged / options holding
- watchlist only
- research only

Use `system/checks/position-role-checklist.md` before changing a holding when the role is unclear.

## Three-Layer Portfolio Model

### Base Layer

Purpose:

- long-term core assets
- account stabilizer
- exposure to high-quality business models and durable trends

Representative assets may include MSFT, GOOGL, NVDA, or broad core exposure.

Tactical trading is allowed only to optimize cost and must not turn long-term holdings into emotional intraday positions.

### Swing Attack Layer

Purpose:

- main account growth engine
- medium-term thesis expression

The swing layer should focus on a small number of high-quality opportunities with clear thesis, mainline strength, relative strength, and sustained catalysts.

If a swing holding consumes too much attention, reduce risk or write a clearer plan.

### Learning / Speculative Layer

Purpose:

- options
- leveraged tools
- event trades
- hand-feel trades
- short-term experiments

This layer is for learning and high-elasticity expression. It must not destabilize the portfolio or override the base and swing layers.

## 1. Worldview

Worldview decides what is worth researching.

Core question:

> What long-term structural changes do I believe are happening?

## 2. Industry Research

Industry research identifies future trends and structural bottlenecks.

Methods:

- First-principles analysis
- Bottleneck analysis
- Supply-chain mapping
- Value-capture analysis
- Historical analogy

## 3. Company Research

Company research identifies value capturers.

Core questions:

- What problem does the company solve?
- Why does it matter now?
- What is its moat?
- Where is value captured?
- What must be true for the thesis to work?
- What evidence would prove the thesis wrong?

## 4. Valuation

Valuation judges what the asset may be worth.

Use multiple lenses:

- Revenue growth
- Margin structure
- Free cash flow potential
- Comparable companies
- Scenario analysis
- Downside protection

## 5. Falsification

Every thesis must include a kill condition.

Core question:

> When am I wrong?

## 6. Position Sizing

Position sizing decides how much risk to take.

Inputs:

- Confidence level
- Downside risk
- Liquidity
- Time horizon
- Portfolio concentration
- Emotional tolerance

## 7. Risk Control

Risk control decides how to survive.

Watch:

- Single-name concentration
- Thesis drift
- Leverage
- Liquidity
- Event risk
- Correlation risk
- Emotional risk

## 8. Execution Discipline

Execution follows the written plan.

Intraday decisions should be limited to predefined triggers.

## 9. Exit Mechanism

Exit happens when:

- Thesis is falsified
- Valuation becomes unreasonable
- Better opportunity appears
- Position exceeds risk limits
- Original time horizon ends
