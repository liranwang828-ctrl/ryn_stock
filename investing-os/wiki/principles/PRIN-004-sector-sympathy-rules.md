## Core Assertion

Sympathy trades require catalyst diffusion logic.

A stock moving in the same sector is not enough.

## Why It Exists

Sector sympathy can create valid opportunities when the catalyst is industry-wide and peers strengthen together.

But mechanically applying sympathy logic creates FOMO, especially after a leader has already moved.

This principle forces the user to distinguish industry diffusion from emotional follow-through.

## Trigger Conditions

Apply this principle when considering a peer or laggard after a leader or sector stock has moved.

Core questions:

- Is the catalyst industry-level, sector-level, or company-specific?
- Does the leader's move logically diffuse to peers?
- Are other stocks actively strengthening, or merely dragged by emotion?
- Does QQQ or the relevant industry ETF support the move?

## Execution Constraints

Sympathy trades are allowed only when:

- the catalyst has industry or sector resonance
- the leader is strong and peers are responding
- the target has chart, volume, and VWAP confirmation
- position size is small
- exit conditions are clear

## What This Principle Forbids

- Buying only because "it also moved"
- Treating a single-company catalyst as sector-wide
- Starting late after the main opening window without new evidence
- Increasing trials because of profit excitement
- Ignoring divergence between leader and peers

## Falsification Or Revision Conditions

Revise this principle when journal and case reviews show which sectors reliably diffuse catalysts and which do not.

Do not generalize one sector's behavior to all sectors.

## Drift Audit

- Was there real catalyst diffusion?
- Was the peer move active or passive?
- Did the user buy because the leader was missed?
- Were chart, volume, and VWAP all present?
- Was the trade sized as a sympathy trade or as a core thesis?
