## Core Assertion

After a first profit-taking action, regret is not a new sell signal.

Follow-up selling must be triggered by predefined structure, not by the feeling of having sold too early.

## Why It Exists

After taking partial profit, a strong continuing move can create regret.

Regret can become a second emotional sell impulse, shifting attention from thesis validity to whether the exact top was captured.

This principle creates a cooling period between profit release and further action.

## Trigger Conditions

Apply this principle when:

- the position is large
- daily profit is significant
- the asset is trending strongly
- the first profit-taking action has happened
- price continues upward after the first sale
- attention shifts from trend validity to "did I sell the high?"

## Execution Constraints

First profit-taking may release psychological pressure.

After first profit-taking, enter a cooling period before discretionary follow-up selling.

During the cooling period, do not sell again because of regret.

Further selling requires a rule trigger, such as:

- VWAP break
- moving take-profit break
- second target reached
- sector weakness
- volume-price structure distortion

## What This Principle Forbids

- Selling again only because the first sale felt early
- Chasing the exact top
- Turning profit protection into emotional liquidation
- Ignoring VWAP, volume, sector, and preset targets
- Letting regret override the written plan

## Falsification Or Revision Conditions

Revise this principle if repeated journal reviews show the cooling period systematically prevents valid planned exits or increases risk.

Do not remove the cooling period because of one missed higher sell point.

## Drift Audit

- Was the follow-up sell triggered by structure or regret?
- Was the original target plan still valid?
- Did the user focus on process quality or exact top capture?
- Was the second sell already planned?
- Did profit size distort judgment?
